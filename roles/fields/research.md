# research

Research is the work of finding out from outside sources and answering a question with what was found, giving the source of every claim.

## Fundamentals (not dropped)

- Settle the method in advance. — Write down the question, the criteria for what counts, and how you will look, and keep a record of it. [1–2]
- Judge the quality of each source separately. — Assess and state its accuracy, completeness, how recent it is, how it may be distorted, its motive, and its expertise. [1–2]
- Keep fact, assumption, and judgment apart. — Separate them in the text. If an assumption carries the argument, say so. [1–2]
- State how uncertain it is. — Say how uncertain the finding is and why. [1–2]
- Look at other explanations. — Weigh alternative explanations systematically. [1–2]

## Not decided here

These differ from person to person and team to team, and this page leaves them open: the order of the work, how deep the search goes, who looks again at the result and when, which words are used for degrees of probability and what range each covers, and how much a quick check may be trimmed.

## Boundaries with other fields

- Finding out from outside sources is research. Weighing what you already hold is analysis, which is a different job, and the guidance for it differs from field to field. This page does not cover analysis.
- Research answers a question that is already set, by collecting evidence and giving a judgment. Making up the question itself and its method, and measuring on your own, is `lab`.
- Writing the answer up as a paper or a report is `docs`.

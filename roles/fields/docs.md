# docs

Docs is the work of writing something for a reader: a paper, a report, or the material for a talk.

## Fundamentals (not dropped)

- The reader comes first. — Settle the reader, the purpose, and the scope first; they decide the structure and the words. [1–4]
- Write the big things first. — Work on the structure and the argument first, then the sentences, then the proofreading. [1–4]
- Write in plain words. — Short words, short clauses, the active voice. [1–4]
- Do not trust your own writing alone. — Step outside the author's eyes: let time pass, read it aloud, or have a colleague read it. [1–4]

## Not decided here

These differ from person to person and team to team, and this page leaves them open: the finer order of revising, how long the outline and the reader profile are, how replies to reviewers are formatted, whether generative AI may be used in review (the venue decides), what counts as finished (the venue and its editor decide), and how talk material is built.

## Boundaries with other fields

- Filling in a form, typing into a submission system, signing, and approving are `admin`. Checking a venue's formatting requirements at the end of a piece of writing touches both.
- A reviewer's comment that needs a new experiment or analysis is `lab` work. Docs takes the result and revises the text.
- The visual design of slides is `design`.

# design

Design is the work of shaping how something looks and is used, so that a person can choose the form it will have.

## Fundamentals (not dropped)

- Start from people and their needs. — Understand the people and what they need; do not assume. [1–4]
- Solve the right problem. — A well-made screen for the wrongly framed problem still fails. [1–4]
- Test in a cheap form before building. — A sketch or a prototype is cheap to throw away, while built code is not. [1–4]
- Narrow down after looking at several options. — Do not settle on the first idea; produce several and narrow from there. [1–4]
- Do it again. — Test, fix, and test again. [1–4]

## Not decided here

These differ from person to person and team to team, and this page leaves them open: the order and the length of the stages, how fidelity is chosen, how a design is reviewed (a critique, a heuristic evaluation, a usability test) and how deep, how many people take part in a test, whether the final design picks one option or combines the good parts of several, and how a design is handed to those who build it.

## Boundaries with other fields

- Checking a finished build against the chosen design overlaps with review and tests in `code`. Building the screen as software is `code`.
- The stages from finding the problem to going live belong to the whole service, not to design alone.
- The visual design of slides for a talk is design. The text on them is `docs`.
